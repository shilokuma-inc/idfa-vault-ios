IDFA Vault for Mac - Notes for App Review

1. SCREEN RECORDING
A screen recording from a physical Mac running the latest macOS is attached. It starts by launching the app and shows unlocking with Touch ID, the explanation that macOS doesn't provide the IDFA to Mac apps, the IDFAs of the user's iPhone and iPad synced through iCloud in the sidebar, copying one and showing it as a QR code, settings, the menu bar item, and locking the app again. No permission prompts appear except Touch ID.
Screen recording protection: to protect the IDFA, the app excludes its windows from screen recording and screen sharing ("Hide During Screen Recording or Mirroring", on by default). For this video it was turned off in the app's Settings (the sliders button at the top right, or IDFA Vault > Settings). If you record the screen while testing, turn it off first.
The app has no account registration, login, user-generated content, paid content, or In-App Purchases.

2. APP TRACKING TRANSPARENCY
The Mac version never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt, at first launch or later, and doesn't track users. macOS doesn't provide the IDFA to apps, so there is nothing to ask permission for. The Mac binary doesn't link the AppTrackingTransparency or AdSupport frameworks, and its Info.plist has no NSUserTrackingUsageDescription.
The prompt appears only in the iPhone and iPad versions, which read the IDFA of that device. App Privacy lists Device ID as used for tracking because App Privacy is shared by all platforms of the app and iOS requires the tracking permission to read the IDFA. The app never uses the IDFA for advertising or tracking.

3. PURPOSE AND TARGET AUDIENCE
IDFA Vault shows advertising identifiers (IDFA) so that the user can copy them. It is for developers who register test devices in advertising SDKs, and for people who are asked for their IDFA by a carrier or a service. On Mac, it shows the IDFAs of the user's own iPhone and iPad.
The app contains no ads, analytics, or third-party SDKs, and has no server. The developer cannot access any user data.
To protect the identifiers, the app always asks for Touch ID (or the Mac's password) at launch and when it returns from the background. The menu bar item asks for Touch ID or the password each time it opens.

4. HOW TO ACCESS THE MAIN FEATURES
No login, account, credentials, or sample files are required.
1. Launch the app and authenticate with Touch ID (or the password).
2. The window shows "Not Available" for this Mac and explains that macOS doesn't provide the IDFA to Mac apps. No tracking prompt appears.
3. To see IDFAs, open IDFA Vault on an iPhone or iPad signed in with the same Apple Account and allow tracking there. Its IDFA appears in the sidebar under "SAME APPLE ID" within about 20 seconds. Select it to see the details, then choose "Copy" (or press Command-C), "Share", or "QR Code".
4. In Settings, change auto-lock, masking, and screen recording protection, or delete all synced data. The lock button next to the Settings button locks the app.
5. The IDFA Vault item in the menu bar lists the same IDFAs, with a copy button for each.

5. EXTERNAL SERVICES
None other than Apple's CloudKit. The Mac version only reads the records that the user's iPhone and iPad saved to the user's own iCloud private database (an app-generated device UUID, the device model name, the platform, the IDFA, and the tracking permission status). It doesn't write a record for the Mac. The public and shared databases are not used.

6. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES
None. The app's features and content are the same in all regions.

7. REGULATED INDUSTRY / THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL
The app is not in a regulated industry and contains no third-party protected material. The biometric marks are Apple's SF Symbols.
