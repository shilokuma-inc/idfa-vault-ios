IDFA Vault for Apple Vision Pro - Notes for App Review

1. SCREEN RECORDING
A screen recording from a physical Apple Vision Pro running the latest visionOS is attached. It starts by launching the app and shows unlocking with Optic ID, the explanation that visionOS doesn't provide the IDFA to apps, the IDFAs of the user's iPhone and iPad synced through iCloud, copying one and showing it as a QR code, settings, and locking the app again. No permission prompts appear except Optic ID.
Screen recording protection: to protect the IDFA, the app covers its window while the screen is recorded or mirrored ("Hide During Screen Recording or Mirroring", on by default). For this video it was turned off in the app's Settings (the sliders button at the top right). If you record the screen while testing, turn it off before you start recording (while recording, the cover also hides Settings).
The app has no account registration, login, user-generated content, paid content, or In-App Purchases.

2. APP TRACKING TRANSPARENCY
The visionOS version never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt, at first launch or later, and doesn't track users. visionOS doesn't provide the IDFA to apps (the visionOS SDK has no AdSupport framework), so there is nothing to ask permission for. The visionOS binary doesn't link the AppTrackingTransparency or AdSupport frameworks, and its Info.plist has no NSUserTrackingUsageDescription.
The prompt appears only in the iPhone and iPad versions, which read the IDFA of that device. App Privacy lists Device ID as used for tracking because App Privacy is shared by all platforms of the app and iOS requires the tracking permission to read the IDFA. The app never uses the IDFA for advertising or tracking.

3. PURPOSE AND TARGET AUDIENCE
IDFA Vault shows advertising identifiers (IDFA) so that the user can copy them. It is for developers who register test devices in advertising SDKs, and for people who are asked for their IDFA by a carrier or a service. On Apple Vision Pro, it shows the IDFAs of the user's own iPhone and iPad.
The app contains no ads, analytics, or third-party SDKs, and has no server. The developer cannot access any user data.
To protect the identifiers, the app always asks for Optic ID (or the device passcode) at launch and when it returns from the background.

4. HOW TO ACCESS THE MAIN FEATURES
No login, account, credentials, or sample files are required.
1. Launch the app and authenticate with Optic ID (or the passcode).
2. The window shows "Not Available" and explains that visionOS doesn't provide the IDFA to visionOS apps. No tracking prompt appears.
3. To see IDFAs, open IDFA Vault on an iPhone or iPad signed in with the same Apple Account and allow tracking there. Its IDFA appears under "SAME APPLE ID" within about 20 seconds (pull down to refresh). Tap a row to show all digits, or use the buttons on the row to copy the IDFA or show it as a QR code.
4. In Settings (the sliders button at the top right), change auto-lock, masking, and screen recording protection, or delete all synced data. The lock button next to it locks the app.

5. EXTERNAL SERVICES
None other than Apple's CloudKit. The visionOS version only reads the records that the user's iPhone and iPad saved to the user's own iCloud private database (an app-generated device UUID, the device model name, the platform, the IDFA, and the tracking permission status). It doesn't write a record for Apple Vision Pro. The public and shared databases are not used.

6. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES
None. The app's features and content are the same in all regions.

7. REGULATED INDUSTRY / THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL
The app is not in a regulated industry and contains no third-party protected material. The biometric marks are Apple's SF Symbols.
